Maria opened the small café on Elm Street in the spring of 2019. At first, only a handful of regulars came by, mostly neighbors who wanted strong coffee and a quiet place to read. She baked every morning before sunrise, and the smell of warm bread soon drifted down the block. By autumn, the line stretched out the door. Some customers brought their laptops; others simply talked for hours. A reporter from the local paper called the place charming but a little naïve about prices. Maria laughed when she read it, because she knew that people would pay more for kindness than for sugar. Still, she raised the price of a croissant by fifty cents. Nobody complained.
